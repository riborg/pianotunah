## Workflow

1. **Survey.** Choose *All A* (A0–A7, about a minute) or *All keys* (3–5 minutes) in the panel that opens. Strike the key shown; the app moves on by itself and stops when every key is done.
2. **Choose the pitch.** If the piano is flat: *Use the piano's current pitch* at A4 = … Hz above the dial, or raise it with overpull.
3. **Mute the unisons** with a felt strip. Tune one string per note and the others by ear against it.
4. **Tune A3–A4 first**, then upwards octave by octave, the bass last.
5. **New pass** next time. The history shows how far the piano has drifted.

**Tuning order** (O) goes through these steps key by key: pitch check, pitch raise, felt strip, temperament by fourths and fifths, treble, bass, with intervals to check by ear and their beat rates. It moves on after two strikes in a row in tune. Other keys and modes can be used meanwhile; it waits on its key.

## Why stretch?

Strings are stiff, so the overtones lie slightly above integer multiples: fₙ = n·f₀·√(1 + B·n²). B, the inharmonicity, is largest in the treble and in short bass strings.

Beatless octaves require the upper note's fundamental to match the lower note's second (fourth, sixth) partial. So the treble is tuned sharp and the bass flat, typically 20–40 cents at the ends.

**Octave type p:q:** partial p of the lower note and partial q of the upper note are made beatless. 2:1 gives the narrowest octave, then 4:2, 6:3, 8:4. Above C6 the treble octaves move to 2:1 by C8, as the upper note's higher partials are weak there.

## Measuring

In the bass, partial 6, 4 or 2 is measured: the fundamental is weak and laptop microphones pick up little below 100 Hz. An external USB microphone 20–40 cm above the strings helps; choose it in Settings (⌘,) under Microphone.

**Auto and Lock:** Auto finds the key from the sound. Keys measured in this pass are also recognised where they sound, even far from the target. An unmeasured key halfway between two notes: click it on the keyboard to lock it.

**Overpull:** when raising the pitch, strings already tuned go flat as the load increases. With 25%, a note that was at −20 cents is tuned to +5. At A4 = … Hz the app suggests an overpull once the middle of the piano is measured.

**Strobe:** one ring per partial behind the needle, numbered at the right end: innermost the measured one, then the two octaves above it. Still = in tune, turning clockwise = too high. If the inner ring stands still but the others turn, the string's inharmonicity differs from the model; measure its inharmonicity under *Partials vs target*.

**Sine tone:** choose *Sine tone* in the menu next to ▶ above the dial (it reads *Piano* until then) and play it with ▶, T or space; ▶ turns into ■ and stops it. It sounds at the partial the tuner measures, so from B♭3 down it lies an octave or more above the key (from B♭1 down, the partial 6 is two octaves and a fifth up); ▶ shows the note it plays. Tune until the beats disappear. *Also play when a key is clicked*, in the same menu, plays the tone when a key on the keyboard is clicked.

**Temperament:** how the fifths are distributed within the octave. Equal temperament is the standard for pianos; the historical ones, under Historical temperaments in the piano's settings (⌥⌘I), are for early music.

*Keyboard: ← → change key, L locks, A auto, O tuning order, T or space plays or stops the target tone. ⌥⌘I shows the piano's settings, ⌘, the app's settings, ⌘? this help.*
