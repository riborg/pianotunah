# Tuning step by step

How to tune a piano with Piano Tunah, following the tuning order (**O**). [Svenska](tuning.sv.md).

## Tools

- Tuning lever with a tip that fits the pins snugly.
- Felt strip (temperament strip), about 2 cm wide and 50–60 cm long.
- Two or three rubber wedges.
- The app on a laptop or phone close to the piano.

## Before you start

1. **Piano tab:** instrument, size, keys (85 or 88) and strings per key. To find the string counts, press the keys slowly from the bass end and count the strings in front of each hammer that moves. Enter the last key with one string and the last key with two. One example: one string up to A1 (key 13), two up to E♭3 (key 31), three above that.
2. **Survey** *All A* or *All keys*. Nothing is tuned yet. The survey measures the strings' inharmonicity and where the piano stands.
3. **Reference:** 440 Hz, or the piano's current pitch if it is old and far flat.

## Why the middle comes first

In equal temperament only the octave is pure. Every other interval is slightly off, in a fixed direction:

| Interval | Off by | Beats, at A4 = 440 |
|---|---|---|
| Fifth | about 2 cents narrow | A3–E4 ≈ 0.7/s |
| Fourth | about 2 cents wide | A3–D4 ≈ 1/s |
| Major third | about 14 cents wide | F3–A3 ≈ 7/s, C4–E4 ≈ 10/s |

So one octave, F3–E4, is laid carefully first. That is the temperament. Everything else is tuned as octaves outwards from it, and octaves are easy to hear. An error in the temperament spreads across the whole piano. The app shows the beat rates for your piano, with its inharmonicity included.

The **felt strip** is there because the strings of a key never agree exactly. Two strings that differ beat against each other, which drowns the slow beats you listen for, and the dial jumps. With the outer strings muted, one string per key sounds.

## Steps

### 1. Check the pitch

The survey shows how far the middle is from the target. Less than 10 cents off: go to step 3.

### 2. Pitch raise

A quick pass over every key and all its strings, without the strip: from A3 up to the top, then from A♭3 down to the bottom, to within a few cents. Overpull tunes each key past the target so the piano settles near it. More than 50 cents flat: do two pitch raises. Old strings break most easily at the pin while they are being raised, so raise them in stages.

### 3. Felt strip F3–A4

Press the strip into the gaps between neighbouring keys' strings, so it pushes the outer strings of each key aside and mutes them. The middle string stays free.

- Upright: between the pressure bar and the hammers. Hold the damper pedal down while the strip goes in, so the dampers do not bend.
- Grand: between the dampers and the front end of the strings.
- Keep it clear of where the hammers strike: push a few hammers forward and look.
- Strike each key. It should sound like one clean string.

### 4. A4 and A3

A4 to the target, then A3 as an octave below it. Check: the tenth F3–A4 beats as fast as the third F3–A3, or slightly faster. If the third is faster, A3 is too high.

### 5. Temperament F3–E4

Eleven keys in fourths and fifths from A3, each tuned from the key before it:

| Key | From | Interval |
|---|---|---|
| D4 | A3 | fourth up |
| G3 | D4 | fifth down |
| C4 | G3 | fourth up |
| F3 | C4 | fifth down |
| B♭3 | F3 | fourth up |
| E♭4 | B♭3 | fourth up |
| A♭3 | E♭4 | fifth down |
| C♯4 | A♭3 | fourth up |
| F♯3 | C♯4 | fifth down |
| B3 | F♯3 | fourth up |
| E4 | B3 | fourth up |

Fourths slightly wide, fifths slightly narrow. Compare each new interval with the same kind of interval below it. The thirds should beat evenly faster the higher they lie, from F3–A3 to C4–E4. A third that breaks the series means a key is off. If the fourths and fifths are tuned too pure, F3–A3 beats too fast.

### 6. Octaves F4–A♭4

Keep the strip in. Tune each key as an octave from the temperament. Check it with the third and tenth, and with the fourth and fifth.

### 7. Treble B♭4 to the top

For each key, put a wedge between strings 2 and 3 so string 1 sounds alone. Tune it as an octave from below. Move the wedge so strings 1 and 2 sound and tune 2 pure against 1, then pull the wedge and tune 3 against both. High up, use short, firm strikes, listen right after the attack, and check against keys two or three octaves down.

### 8. Pull the strip, A4 down to F3

One key at a time: free the outer strings and tune them pure against the middle one.

### 9. Bass, E3 down to A0

Two keys at a time. Wedge so one string sounds, tune it as an octave from above, then tune the other strings against it. Keys with one string have no unison to tune. Use longer strikes and let the note ring. In the deep bass the twelfth and the double octave tell more than the octave, and the beats get slower further down.

### 10. Finish

Firm strikes across the keyboard, then play through the circle of fifths and listen. Tune the keys the app lists as outside tolerance again.

## Tips

- Bring each string up to pitch from below. If you overshoot, go below the target and come up again.
- Set the pin so the string stays put: strike firmly a few times and check that the reading holds.
- If the dial jumps on a key, its strings beat against each other. Mute all but one.
- Pins that turn too easily or jump point to a worn pin block. That is a job for a piano technician.
